# Decision relay: a run suspends on the parent's answer without a rule leaving the core

Accepted for `engine-mvp-decision-relay` under the [engine charter](../ENGINE-CHARTER.md). It is the protocol half that `think:359` split from [one executable decision interface](engine-decisions.md), delivered after [runtime support enforcement](engine-mvp-support-runtime.md), whose terminal `resolution_choice_unsupported` answer it replaces with a suspension. The research finding and plan are `think:416` and `think:417`; the implementation findings are `think:418`. Native Magistr records hold execution status.

The [source pin](../../engine/source.json) advances to canonical `mtg-game-engine` commit `7e740bc3dd51a7676d0e411e5a8bd54a3249c9fe` on `main`. The root `Cargo.lock` is byte-identical. The worker contract is revision 6: the schema digest, both generated bindings and the fixtures change; MCP's imported copies are synced from the clean clone and checked by the ordinary drift test.

## Scope

Every decision the canonical loop asks of a seat, at priority and for a pending request, arrives through one call on the seat's client, and the loop executes the returned action through `submit_decision_controlled`. The worker's two engine-side clients answered priority themselves and could answer nothing else: a resolution request, a command-zone offer or a waiting commander move ended the run as unsupported, and the contract's `Decision` and `DecisionRequest` shapes were placeholders (an option-id list with no kind, no identities and no way to decline). The worker advertised `decisions: false`. This unit makes a run suspend on such a request, present it to the parent as a finite list of opaque options, execute the option the parent names, and continue; it adds no rule and interprets no option.

## Ownership and reuse review

- **One decision owner, one executor.** The engine enumerates the options and executes the answer; the worker presents and relays; the TypeScript service relays and records. No option is interpreted anywhere outside the core: the parent names an `option_id`, the relay maps it to the `Action` the enumerator spelled, and the loop executes it through the same surface every engine-side client uses, rejecting it as it would any client's.
- **The suspension reuses the run's rendezvous.** Progress already crossed the run thread and the session through one channel with an acknowledgement; a suspension crosses the same channel and waits on an answer instead of an acknowledgement. Output accounting, sequence numbering, cancellation and deferral are the existing session mechanics, extended by one item kind.
- **Identities come from the setup owner.** The relay reads `oracle_id` and `physical_id` from the prepared inventory the [catalog-objects](engine-mvp-catalog-objects.md) unit established; a land play names no card because the engine chooses the land; a generated object or an injected object carries none.
- **A decline is neither a cancel nor a loss.** The charter keeps voluntary choices, cancellation and caps distinct: a declined request ends the run as `unsupported/decision_declined` with the request's identities, a cancel while suspended is `cancelled`, an expired budget is `truncated`.
- **The MCP tool does not grow a second decision path.** Exposing decisions to real MCP clients belongs to `engine-mvp-mcp-workflow`; the tool keeps its two engine-side clients and refuses the relay client, while the service proves the relay against the real binary through its own schema.
- **Overlap.** None added. `NamedPolicy::agent` was replaced by `engine_client`, its only caller being the driver.

## Rules basis

The relay presents and executes only what the core already decides. The options at priority are the enumerator's spelling of the canonical decision surface (CR 117.1 and 117.3 govern who may act and when; the enumerator answers that, not the parent). A pending request is the engine's own question (CR 608.2 for decisions during resolution; CR 903.9a and 903.9b for the command-zone offer and the waiting commander move), answered through the same controlled submission the engine-side clients use, so a stale or forged answer is rejected without touching the canonical bytes (CR 733.1 through the transactional executor). Read from the engine's retained snapshot (`crates/mtg-engine/src/cards/support/preview_sources/comprehensive-rules.txt`).

## Limits

- Combat declarations (attackers and blockers) are not on the decision surface, so a `parent-relay` seat attacks with every legal attacker and never blocks, as the engine's default declarations do; `engine-mvp-combat-objects` owns moving them onto the surface, after which the relay presents them like any other decision.
- The MCP tool relays nothing: a single tool call has no decision client. `engine-mvp-mcp-workflow` exposes decisions to real MCP clients; `engine-mvp-runtime-checkpoint` persists a suspended request across the boundary.
- A relayed request presents the enumerator's bounded sample (256 declarations, X up to 16) exactly as the engine-side clients see it; answers outside the sample are not representable on the wire, which `engine-any-choice-forms` records.
- The option bound (1,024) is proven through a smaller bound in-process; no fixture state reaches it.
- One relay per run: a request is answered by exactly one `decision` frame, and the parent sees no hidden information beyond the seat's own legal options and the names of the cards they name.
