## What remained and what was delivered

Every request kind already had a canonical owner: mode and target announcement (`cast_requests`, `ability_requests`), mana declarations (`ManaChoiceRequest` from `CastingOptions` or `ActivatedAbility`), selected physical costs (`PhysicalCostRequest`), post-cost mana outputs (`mana_output_options`), and resumable resolution and ward requests (`pending_choice`). What no consumer could do was present them as one thing and answer them through one door: callers assembled five request calls and six entry points, the cast declaration path could not carry selected or additional costs, and a mana output activation had no state guard, so a stale output choice was the one decision the engine did not reject.

The new `mtg_engine::decisions` module is that one presentation and one submission. It composes the owners above and adds no rule of its own.

## Rules basis

From the pinned Comprehensive Rules corpus (SHA-256 `4381ad1b…`, effective 2026-08-07): CR 601.2b–h order announcement (modes, targets, X and other declarations) before total cost determination and payment; CR 602.2 applies the same sequence to activated abilities; CR 605.3 resolves a mana ability immediately after its cost is paid; CR 608.2 governs decisions made during resolution; CR 702.21a and 700.14 fix ward and expend as delivered by the previous unit; CR 603.3a fixes a triggered ability's controller at the event that fired it, which is why the surface accepts the controller the scheduler supplies rather than re-deriving it from the source's current controller.

## Architecture and reuse review

| Concern               | Owner                       | Change                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| --------------------- | --------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Decision presentation | `decisions.rs` (new)        | `DecisionRequest` is `Resolution`, `Cast`, `Activation`, `Trigger` or `ManaOutput`. `cast_request`, `activation_request`, `trigger_request` and `mana_output_request` describe one source; `pending_decision` wraps the continuation owner; `decision_requests` lists everything one player may be asked or could declare in the current step, with a pending resolution request as the only entry for its deciding player and nothing for anyone else. Mana output requests carry `outputs: None` until every selected cost has been chosen. |
| Decision submission   | `decisions.rs`              | `DecisionResponse` is executed by `submit_choice`, `cast_spell_with_declaration_and_costs`, `activate_ability_with_declaration_and_cost_choices`, `push_triggered_ability_with_declaration` or `activate_mana_output`. `DecisionError` lifts every owner's `ChoiceError::Stale` to one `Stale` so a caller re-describes instead of inspecting each owner. Nothing in the module mutates state itself.                                                                                                                                         |
| Cast with costs       | `casting/mod.rs`            | `cast_spell_with_declaration_and_costs` combines a state-bound declaration (modes, bound targets, mana declarations) with `CastingOptions` (additional and alternative costs, selected physical objects) in the existing cast transaction. `cast_spell_with_declaration` is now its wrapper with default costs.                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| Mana output guard     | `decisions.rs`              | A mana output response carries the canonical state hash and is rejected as stale before `activate_mana_output` runs, matching every other decision.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| Timing                | `turn/action_enumerator.rs` | The active-player, main-phase, empty-stack predicate is factored into `can_act_at_sorcery_speed` and shared by `enumerate_actions` and `decision_requests`; `cast_requires_sorcery_speed` became crate-visible. Priority itself stays with the scheduler.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |

No worker protocol, snapshot schema, support registry, Oracle parser, TypeScript gameplay path or public MCP route changed. The worker still advertises `decisions: false`; relaying `DecisionRequest` through the protocol belongs to the unified decision interface and worker chunks. The CLI console agent presents casts by filtering `enumerate_actions`, so it implements no rules of its own and needed no migration; moving the enumerator and agents onto the surface is recorded on `engine-mvp-decisions`.

## Independent evidence

Canonical `tests/cost_choices.rs` holds nine scenarios written before the module existed (compilation failed on the absent module). Each names the frozen corpus branch it exercises with typed fixtures that represent one clause and certify no card:

- Witherbloom Command (`7cc81fb3…`): the cast request exposes the choose-two mode request before any target request; with modes supplied the -3/-1 target request lists the opponent's creature; a printed-order violation is rejected byte-identically; the accepted cast retains the announced modes, spends exactly `{B}{G}` and resolves to -1/1 plus two life.
- Kairi, the Swirling Sky (`85aa53e8…`): after the opponent's targeting spell is cast through the surface and the ward trigger is placed, nothing is pending until resolution; the ward request then appears through `pending_decision` and in the targeting player's `decision_requests` only, the ward's controller sees nothing, the controller's answer is rejected, and `Yes` pays three mana without counting as casting spend.
- Tasigur, the Golden Fang (`837e3fde…`) and Trailtracker Scout (`d38646dc…`): the activation request exposes two hybrid symbol choices; the accepted activation spends `{2}{G}{U}` and counts nothing toward expend; two surface casts of five and three cross expend 8 exactly at the eighth mana.
- Pernicious Deed (`62e44e0d…`): the activation request requires X and needs no selection for the self-sacrifice; a declaration made before mana was added is rejected as `Stale` with identical bytes; the current declaration sacrifices the enchantment and retains X on the stack.
- Molt Tender (`9a39ca64…`): the whole presentation lists the source with its graveyard-exile request and no outputs; with the own card selected all five colors are offered, an opponent's card is refused, a forged two-mana output is rejected, the chosen blue is produced with the card exiled and the source tapped, and replaying the same response is `Stale`.
- Reclamation Sage (`032ec6e2…`) versus Fleshbag Marauder (`4b1bf05e…`): the trigger request exposes the optional target slot and a declaration without it is rejected; the resolved request reports `Optional` with `No` and `Yes`; the sacrifice request reports `Mandatory` with exactly one object and an empty answer is rejected.
- Kicker plus a selected discard travel with one declaration; the spell is never a discard candidate; a missing selection rejects before anything is paid; the accepted cast is kicked, discards the chosen card and counts three mana toward expend.
- Foreign and forged responses of every kind (another player's cast, a graveyard card as a creature target, another player's activation, an undeclared hybrid symbol, a stale trigger declaration, a trigger declaration carrying mana, another player's mana source, a stale mana output, a forged resolution request) preserve bytes and the event log.
- The presentation follows timing: a sorcery-speed cast disappears with a non-empty stack while a direct request still describes it, the opponent sees nothing, and a pending resolution suspends every other request.

Three temporary mutations each fail at least one scenario: removing the mana-output stale guard fails two; removing the deciding-player filter from the presentation fails the Kairi scenario; dropping the supplied costs from the declaration cast fails the kicker-plus-discard scenario.

## Boundaries and retained work

The surface presents the request kinds the engine represents today: modes, single-slot targets, X, hybrid, monohybrid and Phyrexian symbols, selected sacrifice, discard and graveyard exile, post-cost mana outputs, optional and ordered resolution steps, object selections, and ward payment. Numeric, name and type choices, divisions, opponent choices, votes and secret commitments remain with `engine-any-choice-forms`. The trigger's controller is asserted by the scheduler that fires it, not by the surface. `decision_requests` applies the enumerator's timing rule and does not know who holds priority; the validator and game loop keep that. The worker protocol does not yet relay `DecisionRequest`; `enumerate_actions`, the heuristic and search agents and the CLI still build their actions from declarations directly, which the unified decision interface (`engine-mvp-decisions`) can now migrate onto this one surface.
