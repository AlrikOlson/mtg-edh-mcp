# Eliminating players without ending the game

Accepted for `engine-mvp-multiplayer-elimination` under the [engine charter](../ENGINE-CHARTER.md). It is the first of three units split from `engine-mvp-multiplayer` (reasoning `think:362`); the plan is `think:363`, the design correction made during implementation is `think:364`, and the loss site found while writing this receipt is `think:365`. Native Magistr records hold execution status.

The [source pin](../../engine/source.json) advances to canonical `mtg-game-engine` commit `14935a451007c9489b9ad9f40a03690706b91aa0` on `main`: the unit is `35e47ec` plus the follow-up fix `14935a4` described below, and every gate and the whole pin qualification were rerun at the final revision. The root `Cargo.lock`, the worker protocol and every generated contract derivative are byte-identical; only the manifest's source revision changes. Preview runs stay pinned to two seats by the contract and full sessions already require four, so lifting the engine's seat gate changes no schema.

## Why the chunk was split

"Support four players" was sized against the code before planning. Seat vectors, turn rotation, the priority round and active-player-first trigger draining already took any number of players. Three separable owners did not:

- **Game lifecycle.** Setup refused anything but two seats, every loss path ended the game and named `find_opponent` the winner, nothing implemented leaving a game, and turn rotation landed on lost players.
- **Combat.** Attack and block declaration use one defender, and the agent combat hooks carry that single defender through the human, heuristic, search, CLI and desktop consumers.
- **Opponent scope.** The heuristic, search, determinizer and rating code evaluate a single opponent, and trigger ordering among several non-active players is untested.

Changing the agent trait, the loop's terminal semantics and the combat model in one commit would have had no independently testable midpoint. The lifecycle unit is delivered here; `engine-mvp-multiplayer-combat` and `engine-mvp-multiplayer-opponents` are pending and depend on it; `engine-mvp-multiplayer` keeps every original acceptance obligation and now depends on all three.

## What was delivered

| ------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |

| `game_loop.rs` `find_opponent`              | chose the winner and the defender                                    | no longer chooses a winner; survives only as `default_defender` until the combat unit replaces it                                                                                                                                                                                         |

| `ZoneManager`, `EventBus`, `EffectRegistry` | per-card removal only                                                | `cards_owned_by`, `discard_pending_controlled_by`, `remove_control_granted_to`; the last goes through the existing mutation facade so control history reconciles                                                                                                                          |

Reuse was the default. Object removal is the existing token-cessation primitive; triggers, replacements, continuous effects and combat participation are dropped through their existing per-card removal functions; stack objects leave through the existing identity-checked removal. No zone, registry or stack path was added beside an existing one.

## Rules basis

Wizards' Comprehensive Rules, 2026-08-07 snapshot. 104.3a–d: conceding, zero life, drawing from an empty library and ten poison counters are losses. 104.2a: a player wins when every opponent has left, and the game ends immediately. 104.4a: if everyone loses at once the game is a draw. 800.4a: when a player leaves a multiplayer game, objects they own leave the game, effects giving them control of objects end, their spells and abilities on the stack cease to exist, and anything they still control is exiled. 800.4j: if the active player leaves, the turn continues without an active player receiving priority.

The ordering of 104.2a before 800.4a matters. The first implementation removed the final loser's objects too, and an existing two-player preview conformance scenario failed because the final position no longer held the loser's hand. The loss that ends the game now leaves the final position intact. This follows the rules rather than a player-count branch, so two-player final states and their hashes are unchanged and a simultaneous multiplayer loss ends in one consistent position.

## Independent evidence

`crates/mtg-engine/tests/multiplayer_elimination.rs` was written before any production change and failed only on the absent module and log entry. Eleven scenarios: two to four seats allocate and zero, one and five are refused; four seats take turns in seat order; empty-library draws eliminate one player a turn until one remains; a non-active loser leaves with their battlefield, graveyard, library and lent objects while a stolen creature returns to its owner, a bystander's permanent stays and the leaver's stack ability never resolves; a conceding active player takes no further action and the turn completes; non-active concessions end the game only at one survivor; simultaneous losses leave the survivor or draw the game; two-player outcomes are unchanged; the ending loss keeps the final position; the owner is idempotent byte for byte and a departed player is never a target; a failed draw inside a resolving effect leaves through the same owner. Five unit tests cover the turn-order and priority index cases, including a second departure during a departed active player's turn.

Five mutations were applied and restored byte for byte. Dropping turn-order removal, owned-object removal, the end of control effects and the game-over guard each failed at least one scenario. Keeping the leaver's stack objects initially survived: the ability resolved harmlessly and the stack still ended empty. The scenario now asserts that nothing resolved, and the mutation fails it.

Recorded gates on engine `main`: workspace check with all targets including the desktop crate, 1,688 tests (3 pre-existing skips) plus 110 doctests, Clippy with pedantic warnings denied, rustfmt. One existing test changed by design: the preview setup refusal now refuses five seats and allocates four. On the pin task: clean-clone identity before and after the locked offline arm64 release worker build, the empty-directory self-check, contract sync and check, 147 native MCP tests against the clean-clone binary, and the 13-test evidence requalification run. At the intermediate revision nextest flagged one passing evidence test as leaking a handle; the rerun at the final pin did not.

## A loss site found while writing this receipt

Checking a claim for a discovery chunk against every `set_lost` call showed that a failed draw inside a resolving spell or ability marks the loss when resolution finishes, without reaching the elimination owner. In a continuing game that player was counted out by the terminal check but stayed in the turn order owning their objects. A scenario was written first and failed with no elimination recorded. `elimination::owed_departures` now lists players marked lost who have not left, and the loop's state-based pass eliminates them through the same owner. It landed as follow-up commit `14935a4` rather than an amended commit, and the first pin at `35e47ec` was re-qualified from the start at `14935a4`. One rerun check was recorded through a pipe, so its exit code belonged to the filter; it was rerun unpiped and that record is the evidence.

## Retained work and known limits

- The two exile branches of the owner (a permanent the leaver still controls after control effects end, and another player's card the leaver was casting) follow CR 800.4a but no scenario reaches them: the engine derives an object's base controller from its owner and has no way yet to cast or put onto the battlefield another player's card under your control. They need fixtures when `engine-mvp-zones` or `engine-mvp-graveyard` makes them reachable.
- Combat still declares against the first living opponent in seat order. `engine-mvp-multiplayer-combat` replaces `default_defender` and the single-defender agent hooks.
- Trigger draining orders the active player first but does not yet order several non-active players by turn order, and the built-in policies evaluate one opponent. Both belong to `engine-mvp-multiplayer-opponents`.
- A resolution choice owed by a departed player who does not control the resolving object is not yet resolved by rule; a repeated concession from such a player is rejected explicitly rather than looping. The pending-stack-decision fixtures remain an obligation of `engine-mvp-multiplayer`.
- The empty-library loss is decided in three places: the draw step, the end of a resolving effect, and the bridge into the elimination owner. Outcomes are correct; `engine-sba-empty-library-loss` moves the decision into the state-based owner and deletes the bridge.
- The worker's self-check and the preview profile still run two seats; four-seat worker and MCP evidence is an obligation of `engine-mvp-multiplayer`.
