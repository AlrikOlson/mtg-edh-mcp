## What remained and what was delivered

The previous unit made `mtg_engine::decisions` present every request kind and execute every response kind, but nothing executed through it. The legal-action enumerator sampled casts and activations from the declaration sampler directly; the game loop called `cast_spell_with_declaration`, `cast_spell`, `activate_ability_with_declaration`, `activate_ability` and `submit_choice_controlled` itself; the heuristic, search and CLI clients answered pending requests through the continuation owner. This unit routes all of them through the one surface and adds no rule.

## Rules basis

From the pinned Comprehensive Rules corpus (SHA-256 `4381ad1b…`, effective 2026-08-07): CR 601.2b–h order announcement before payment, which is why a legacy target list binds by position to the announced slots and never supplies modes or mana; CR 602.2 applies the same sequence to activations; CR 603.3 places a triggered ability without paying a cost, so trigger declarations sample under a zero-mana request; CR 605.3 resolves a mana ability immediately, so a mana output response is a decision without a stack object; CR 608.2 governs decisions during resolution, which the enumerator answers only for the deciding player.

## Independent evidence

Canonical `tests/decision_interface.rs` holds seven scenarios written before any production change (compilation failed on the four absent surface functions):

- The enumerator's cast and activation actions are exactly the surface's candidates, in order, carrying the presentation's state guard; the opponent sees none of them; standalone mana outputs stay out of the agent enumeration.
- An unbound default cast keeps its `CastSpell` spelling and its legacy translation is the empty declaration under the current guard, which the surface executes.
- Legacy target lists bind by the announcement: a graveyard card, a missing target, an extra target and a missing ability are rejected without touching the canonical bytes; the bound cast and activation execute and resolve.
- The loop executes legacy `CastSpell` and `ActivateAbility` actions through the surface (the opponent ends at 16 life and the client's own spelling is what the log records).
- A client written only against the surface drives the loop to a resolved cast, and a stale declaration is rejected with the spell still in hand, an empty stack, untouched life and no logged action.
- The heuristic, search (one candidate, one rollout) and random clients answer a mandatory pending discard with the pending request itself, each answer executes through the controlled submit, and the enumerator's candidates for that request are exactly its valid selections.
- Trigger and mana output requests expand to responses the surface accepts; a mana source whose selected cost is unchosen samples nothing until it is chosen.

Four temporary mutations each fail at least one scenario and were reversed: dropping the announced-option check from the legacy binding fails the legacy scenario; dropping resolution answers from the enumerator fails the mandatory-pending scenario through the random client; dropping sorcery timing from the presentation fails the enumerator's existing sorcery test; dropping the affordability probe from the cast sampler fails the search agent's multi-seed benchmark.

Performance was measured, not assumed. The first cut presented every controlled source's post-cost mana outputs on every priority pass and hashed eagerly, which pushed the search agent's bounded decision to 1.70 s against its 500 ms budget. With the priority-actions filter and the lazy guard the enumerator costs 13.9 ms per call on an 80-card state, identical to the clean clone at the previous pin, and the bounded decision is back under budget.

## Split and retained work

The chunk record listed three remaining items. This unit delivers the engine-internal one. The other two are each cross-repository and independently testable, so under the charter's split-before-implement rule they become pending chunks that keep this chunk's obligations:

- `engine-mvp-decision-relay`: relay `DecisionRequest` and `DecisionResponse` through the worker protocol. The worker still advertises `decisions: false`; a run cannot yet suspend on a request and wait for the parent's frame, and the contract needs a revision with regenerated bindings and the drift check. Runtime checkpointing of pending decisions and the full MCP session workflow depend on it.
- `engine-mvp-payment-conformance`: turn the independent MCP mana and payment reference (`src/analyze/manaSequence.reference.test.ts` and its fixture semantics) into conformance inputs the canonical engine reads, with the multi-source and ability cases the roadmap acceptance names, without copying production TypeScript payment logic. Corpus mana lands and the legacy cutover depend on it.

Within the engine, the sampler is bounded (256 declarations, X up to 16) and `declarations_for` remains behind the surface; the requests stay authoritative for answers outside the sample, which is the extension point `engine-any-choice-forms` records. Loyalty activations are still not presented (retained on `engine-mvp-planeswalkers`), and the desktop human agent still reads `enumerate_actions`, which now is the surface.
