# Commander play: the five rule units integrated

Accepted for `engine-mvp-commander` under the [engine charter](../ENGINE-CHARTER.md). It is the parent of the five Commander rule units split at `think:386` ([setup](engine-commander-setup.md), [casting and tax](engine-commander-cast-tax.md), [zone return](engine-commander-zone-return.md), [zone replacement](engine-commander-zone-replacement.md), [commander damage](engine-commander-damage.md), [identity-bound mana](engine-commander-identity-mana.md)). The research finding and plan are `think:404`; the implementation findings are `think:405`. Native Magistr records hold execution status.

The [source pin](../../engine/source.json) advances to canonical `mtg-game-engine` commit `1871ac094e59abc5801c76fcd9e36ef4c416678f` on `main`. The root `Cargo.lock` and every generated contract derivative except the synced README are byte-identical; the worker contract schema is unchanged.

## Scope

The five units left three consumers behind: the worker answered `commander_admission_unavailable` before looking at a Commander scenario, the desktop dealt its preset commander into the opening hand through its own builder, and nothing had proven the units together in one four-owner pod. Research found one constraint that shapes what this unit can honestly claim: under `SupportProfile::Commander` the canonical qualifier (`cards::support::assess`) adds unqualified physical-adapter, shared-rules and per-clause requirements to every card, so no card in the registry is supported today and no real deck can reach setup through the worker. The unit therefore delivers the whole admission path up to and including the first canonical refusal, proves the seat translation with test-built definitions, and leaves the starting procedure to its recorded owner instead of inventing one.

## Ownership and reuse review

- **One owner per decision.** Binding and variant policy live in the worker adapter, where the fixture binding already lives; qualification stays in `cards::support`; physical setup stays in `setup::build_game`; the starting procedure stays with `engine-mvp-mulligans`. The worker never calls the permissive card factory or a construction validator, and nothing in it decides a rule.
- **Construction legality is not executability.** A captured deck that MCP validated as legal is still refused per card by the qualifier, and the variants MCP admits for construction (partner, background, Doctor's companion, a companion, a meld commander) are refused as `unsupported` rather than assumed executable (CR 903.3b, 702.139). Those obligations are recorded on the pending any-deck units, not closed here.
- **No invented starting procedure.** `build_game` takes the hand it is given; drawing seven, shuffling and mulligans were assigned to `engine-mvp-mulligans` when the setup unit was accepted. A fully qualified pod is therefore refused at exactly that point with `starting_procedure_unavailable`, after its seats were translated, instead of being dealt by the adapter with an empty hand or a made-up deal. The refusal is reachable in a worker unit test through test-built definitions; in production it is unreachable until the qualifier supports a whole deck.
- **The desktop is a consumer of the setup owner.** Its two game builders now share `prepare_game`; the removed builder was the last production path that put a commander into a hand. The desktop keeps its own per-player shuffle of the blueprint, which is presentation ordering of the same input the starting-procedure owner will later shuffle canonically.
- **Overlap.** None added. The worker's Commander path and the desktop path both end in `build_game`; the preview fixture path is untouched.

## Evidence

### Integrated mirror pod

`crates/mtg-engine/tests/commander_play.rs`: seven scenarios through `build_game` and the real owners, four seats with identical commanders, hands and libraries. They pass against the delivered units, which is the expected outcome of an integration suite over finished owners.

1. Every owner starts with their own commander in the command zone apart from a seven-card hand and the library, at 40 life, with the catalog identity; 80 distinct physical cards come from two shared definitions.
2. One owner's cast, death, offer, return and taxed recast leave the other three owing nothing; two commanders dying at once are answered by their own owners, and declining leaves one in its graveyard while the other returns.
3. Damage totals are kept per commander and per defender through one real combat plus the combat owner's events; 21 from one commander loses only that defender while another defender at 20 from a different commander stays.
4. Counters and land drops belong to one owner and the untap step resets only the active player's.
5. A leaving owner takes only their own designation, cast history and totals.
6. An eight-turn random-agent run of the pod, with the multiplayer first draw, keeps every designation, charges exactly twice the recorded casts as tax, and replays to the same state hash.
7. The integrated state survives a restore round trip.

Per-type graveyard permissions are not claimed: they do not exist yet (`engine-mvp-graveyard`).

### Worker admission

`crates/mtg-worker/tests/commander.rs`, written first and red until the owner existed: a bound mirror pod over the synthetic catalog fixture is refused with exactly one `card_unsupported` diagnostic per distinct Oracle identity in deck order and none per seat or copy; five binding mismatches, a stale implementation version and digest and a mismatched catalog are `invalid`; seven ways of not playing the captured deck are `invalid`; partner, background, Doctor's companion, two commanders under a single kind, a meld commander, a companion and an unknown policy are each refused as a whole before any card is qualified; a 300-identity deck stops at 256 diagnostics in deck order; a stale digest refuses a single-identity deck before its verdict; and, with test-built definitions, `seats` translates four seats to command-zone blueprints with empty hands and 99-card libraries in declared order before `admit_qualified` answers `starting_procedure_unavailable`. The schema fixture's Commander admission, whose catalog carries placeholder bytes, now answers `invalid/catalog_invalid` in the worker and native MCP suites.

## Limits

- No real Commander deck can be admitted or run until the qualifier supports whole cards (`engine-mvp-corpus`, `engine-mvp-support-runtime`) and the starting procedure exists (`engine-mvp-mulligans`, which now carries the worker hook). The worker retains no Commander admission and `run` cannot name one.
- A 903.4b pre-game color choice is not detected or represented; the established identity is the catalog record's, as accepted in the setup unit.
- Every seat plays the one captured deck; independently authored decks per seat need a catalog that carries several decks (`engine-any-pod-inputs`).
- The heuristic and search agents still answer every command-zone offer yes and ignore commander damage totals (`engine-mvp-policies`).
- The engine workspace test gate first failed on the pre-existing wall-clock `search_decision_time_is_bounded` test under parallel load (515 ms against a 500 ms budget, unrelated to this change, seen once before); it passed on the rerun and is recorded as pending `engine-search-decision-time-test-budget`.
